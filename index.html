Executive summary

I’m a Computer Engineering student at Oakton College preparing to transfer into Computer Engineering at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, and I want to build the hardware that flies. My goal is a career in embedded and avionics engineering: the flight computers, sensors, and data networks that keep a vehicle under control.

Through the NCAS Student Challenge I focused on two parts of NASA1. In the Human Spaceflight Mission Directorate, Orion’s vehicle management computer runs the guidance, navigation, control, and propulsion flight software2. In the Research and Technology Mission Directorate, aeronautics research includes the N3-X, a turboelectric aircraft concept driven by 16 electric fans3.

My coursework builds toward that work. C++ and Data Structures taught me to write firmware, Computer Architecture showed me how processors like Orion’s ruggedized flight computers execute instructions4, and Physics I and II and Calculus III gave me the circuits, electromagnetism, and vector math behind motors and navigation. Outside class, I built an ESP32-S3 device with an automated test suite, and I lead Oakton’s Computer Science Club and serve as IEEE treasurer.

The NASA role I’m working toward is Aerospace Engineer, AST – Flight Systems Engineer, with an avionics focus5. Next: the UIUC transfer, PCB design, real-time embedded programming, and NASA Pathways and OSTEM internships6.

Experience and projects

ESP32-S3 Serial Test Harness

Personal project, Sep 2026 – presentgithub.com/DhunP1337/esp32-test-harness

What I did

I wrote C++ firmware for an ESP32-S3 microcontroller that implements a serial command protocol to control and read hardware I/O: an LED, a push button, and a potentiometer. I then built an automated Python and pytest verification suite with functional, negative, stress, and soak tests that check responses, error handling, and stability under thousands of rapid commands. Each run logs per-command latency to CSV and produces an HTML test report, so results are documented across test cycles. The project taught me to design hardware so it can be tested, and to catch failures automatically instead of by hand.

How it aligns with NASA

NASA flight systems engineers manage systems through design, integration, and testing5. Orion’s avionics team credits its first flight test’s success to the large amount of integration and testing done in its development labs4. My test harness is a small version of the same discipline: define what the hardware must do, then prove it automatically. It’s the foundation I want to build on with PCB design and real-time firmware.

Through high school, I designed and built roughly 100 custom PCs, averaging three builds a month, each fit to the client’s budget and needs. How it aligns with NASA

Orion’s flight computers were adapted from computers built for the Boeing 787 and ruggedized for space4, a reminder that picking and qualifying the right parts is real engineering. This business is where I first learned that hands-on hardware work is what I want to do.
